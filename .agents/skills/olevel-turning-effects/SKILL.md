# O Level Turning Effects

## Apply the chapter conventions

- Cover only:
  - T4.1 Moments and the principle of moments;
  - T4.2 Centre of gravity;
  - T4.3 Stability.
- Use

\[
M=F d_\perp
\]

  where \(d_\perp\) is the shortest distance from the pivot to the force's line
  of action.
- Measure force in newtons, perpendicular distance in metres, and moment in
  newton metres, written \(\mathrm{N\,m}\).
- Use \(g=10\ \mathrm{N\,kg^{-1}}\) when converting mass to weight unless the
  question explicitly gives another value.
- Use algebra and O Level force reasoning only. Do not introduce calculus,
  rotational inertia, angular acceleration, vector products, or three-dimensional
  torque.

## Enforce the O Level geometry boundary

- Never resolve a force into horizontal, vertical, parallel, or perpendicular
  components.
- Never use sine, cosine, tangent, inverse trigonometry, or a trigonometric
  projection.
- Never calculate \(d_\perp\) from a force angle, lever angle, sloping length, or
  expression such as \(r\sin\theta\).
- Do not include a numerical non-right angle or an angle arc as assessable data.
  A right-angle marker may show that two lines are perpendicular.
- For a vertical force, use a directly shown horizontal separation. For a
  horizontal force, use a directly shown vertical separation.
- If an oblique force or sloping lever is shown, restrict the geometry task to
  identifying which already-labelled segment is the perpendicular distance.
  Do not use the oblique angle in a calculation.
- If a moment calculation follows a distance-identification part, state or label
  the numerical value of the chosen perpendicular segment directly. Never make
  the student derive it from another length.

Identify \(d_\perp\) in this order:

1. Locate the pivot.
2. Draw or extend the complete line of action of the force.
3. Find the shortest segment from the pivot that meets that line at \(90^\circ\).
4. Select that labelled segment, not the distance from the pivot to the point
   where the force is applied.

## Choose an approved question type

Vary generated questions across the following types. Increase difficulty through
more steps or more forces, never through trigonometry or force resolution.

### 1. Moment concepts and units

- Define the moment of a force.
- State or recognise the unit \(\mathrm{N\,m}\).
- Identify the pivot or line of action.
- Recognise that a force whose line of action passes through the pivot has zero
  moment.

### 2. Perpendicular-distance identification

- Show several labelled candidate lengths such as \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\).
- Ask which one is the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force's line
  of action.
- Show enough exact geometry to decide; do not ask the student to measure the
  drawing or calculate a projection.

### 3. Direction and size of one moment

- Identify clockwise or anticlockwise turning effect.
- Calculate \(M\), \(F\), or a directly given \(d_\perp\).
- Compare how changing the force or perpendicular distance changes the moment.

### 4. Resultant moment

- Calculate clockwise and anticlockwise moments separately.
- Find the resultant moment and its direction.
- Use only forces whose perpendicular distances are directly supplied.

### 5. Principle of moments

- Use a balanced beam, metre rule, seesaw, mobile, wheel-and-axle, or similar
  system.
- Equate total clockwise and total anticlockwise moments about a stated pivot.
- Find an unknown force, weight, or directly measured position.

### 6. Complete static equilibrium

- Use both zero resultant moment and zero resultant force when both conditions
  are required.
- Choose a pivot that removes an unknown reaction from the moment equation, then
  use a one-dimensional force balance if needed.
- Keep all forces already vertical, horizontal, or parallel. Do not create a
  component-resolution problem.
- Do not claim that zero resultant moment alone proves complete equilibrium.

### 7. Centre of gravity

- Define centre of gravity as the point through which the whole weight of an
  object may be considered to act.
- Locate the centre of gravity of a uniform regular object or uniform rod.
- Explain or sequence the plumb-line method for an irregular lamina.
- Use directly supplied support readings and distances with the principle of
  moments to locate the centre of gravity of a non-uniform object.
- Do not assume that every object's centre of gravity is at its geometrical
  centre.

### 8. Stability and toppling

- Trace the vertical line of action of weight through the centre of gravity.
- Decide whether that line lies inside the base, through its edge, or outside it.
- Explain stable, limiting, or toppling behaviour using the base edge as the
  possible pivot.
- Compare designs using a lower centre of gravity or a wider base.
- Treat a just-lifting support reaction as zero in a limiting-equilibrium
  question.

### 9. Practical turning-effect applications

- Explain why a door handle is far from the hinge, why a long spanner is useful,
  or why a force is most effective when its line of action gives a larger
  perpendicular distance.
- Ask for qualitative comparison or use directly labelled distances.

## Set format and difficulty without leaving the syllabus

- Treat **Section A as the multiple-choice section**. Use it to calibrate MCQ
  stem length, four-option structure, and misconception-based distractors. Do
  not use "Section A" as a synonym for easy; Section A can contain MCQs at
  different cognitive difficulties.
- Treat **easy** as foundation level: definitions, units, pivot and direction
  recognition, one direct moment, labelled-distance identification, or a basic
  centre-of-gravity or base judgement. Use one main step and normally 1–3 marks
  for a written question.
- Treat **medium** as Section B structured level: use a clearly scaffolded
  context with a stated pivot and direct perpendicular distances. Ask for a
  reverse \(M=Fd_\perp\) calculation, two opposing moments, a uniform object's
  weight at its midpoint, one support force, or a short stability explanation.
  Normally allocate 3–6 marks.
- Treat **hard** as Section C free-response level: use one coherent, data-rich
  context with reduced scaffolding, linked subparts, and normally 7–12 marks.
- Require every hard non-MCQ question to contain:
  1. at least one explicit **calculate**, **determine**, or **show that** part;
  2. at least one explicit **explain**, **justify**, or **evaluate** part;
  3. a later part that uses, checks, compares, or interprets an earlier result.
- Make the hard calculation preferably a principle-of-moments problem. Make
  selecting the correct equation difficult through physics modelling, not
  trigonometry.
- Require a hard T4 question to use both independent equilibrium conditions:
  - **rotational equilibrium:** total clockwise moment equals total
    anticlockwise moment;
  - **translational equilibrium:** resultant force is zero.
- Keep every perpendicular distance direct and explicit. Do not increase
  difficulty through force resolution, angles, trigonometry, larger numbers, or
  advanced mathematics.

## Build Section C hard questions

Use this sequence:

1. Present one apparatus with several vertical or horizontal forces, including
   at least one support reaction and, where appropriate, the weight of a uniform
   body acting at its midpoint.
2. Ask the learner to draw or complete the forces and select a suitable pivot.
   Do not always state the moment equation or the best pivot.
3. Make the learner determine the true lever arms from labelled straight-line
   positions or length differences. Never require a projection or measurement
   from the drawing.
4. Use the principle of moments about the selected pivot to calculate one
   unknown weight, force, position, or support reaction.
5. Use vertical or horizontal force balance to calculate a second support
   reaction, pivot force, or tension.
6. Follow the calculation with an explanation or judgement about moving a load,
   moving a support, changing the centre of gravity, removing an object,
   approaching toppling, or checking an experimental result.

Choose and rotate among these Section C patterns:

- **Supported beam or plank:** include self-weight and one or more loads; choose
  a pivot to eliminate one reaction, calculate the other reaction from moments,
  use \(\sum F=0\) for the remaining reaction, then explain a position change.
- **Non-uniform plank or vehicle:** use support readings and force balance,
  locate the centre of gravity with moments, then justify a stability or design
  change.
- **Wheelbarrow, cupboard, stroller, or travel case:** use force balance and
  moments to compare or calculate forces, then explain why a changed handle,
  support, or centre-of-gravity position changes the required force.
- **Experimental ruler or mobile:** solve linked or nested moment equations,
  use translational equilibrium for a thread or pivot force, then explain the
  effect of removing a load or diagnose a discrepancy.
- **Limiting toppling:** identify the new pivot edge and zero support reaction,
  calculate the limiting condition, use force balance to determine a remaining
  reaction or applied force, then explain how a lower centre of gravity or wider
  base changes stability.

The difficulty should come from deciding which forces produce moments, which
reaction disappears from the chosen pivot equation, where a uniform body's
weight acts, and when to switch from \(\sum M=0\) to \(\sum F=0\). Do not supply
the final equilibrium equation in the question unless writing it is itself the
assessed task.

For a batch of T4 questions, avoid repeating the same type or apparatus in
adjacent questions. When the batch has at least three T4 questions, include at
least one moments question, one perpendicular-distance diagram question, and one
centre-of-gravity or stability question.

## Draw diagrams that test the intended idea

- Make the pivot, force arrow, full line of action, and every candidate or given
  distance visually distinct.
- Use exact SVG labels for forces, lengths, numbers, units, and right-angle
  markers. Keep an illustrative ImageGen base free of all assessable data.
- Do not use angle arcs, degree labels, or component arrows.
- Mark whether a distance is measured from the pivot or from an end of the
  object. Place the weight of a uniform object at its midpoint.
- For stability, show both base edges, the centre of gravity, the vertical line
  of action of weight, and the contact edge that can become the pivot.
- State every required value. Never require measuring a drawing that is not
  explicitly to scale.
- Keep alternative text accurate without revealing which candidate length or
  option is correct.

## Structure solutions and marking

1. State the pivot and identify the relevant perpendicular distance.
2. State whether each moment is clockwise or anticlockwise.
3. Write \(M=Fd_\perp\), or equate total clockwise and anticlockwise moments.
4. Substitute directly supplied distances in metres.
5. Give the result with unit and direction where applicable.
6. For equilibrium, separately check force balance when the question requires
   complete equilibrium.
7. For stability, connect centre of gravity to the vertical weight line, the
   base, and the possible pivot edge.

Award independent marks for selecting the correct perpendicular distance,
setting up the moment equation, substitution, final value with unit, and a
syllabus-appropriate explanation. Accept equivalent correct wording.
