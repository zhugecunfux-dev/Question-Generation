# O Level Newtonian Mechanics

## Apply the chapter conventions

- Use \(g=10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) throughout. Do not silently replace it with \(9.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).
- Keep all explanations and solutions within O Level scope.
- Do not use differentiation, integration, or calculus notation.

## Teach and solve kinematics

- Treat the area between a velocity–time graph and the time axis as displacement.
- Find distance by adding the magnitudes of the areas; do not cancel areas below the time axis.
- Use elementary shapes such as rectangles, triangles, and trapezia to calculate graph areas.
- Include reverse variants, for example when an area or displacement is given and time must be found.
- For harder variants, define the unknown clearly, express the relevant graph dimensions or quantities in terms of it, form an equation, and solve it algebraically.

## Handle the five constant-acceleration equations

Require students to remember only:

\[
a=\frac{v-u}{t}
\]

Do not require memorisation of the other four standard equations:

\[
s=\frac{u+v}{2}t,\qquad
s=ut+\frac12at^2,\qquad
s=vt-\frac12at^2,\qquad
v^2=u^2+2as
\]

Prefer a syllabus-aligned primary solution using \(a=(v-u)/t\), velocity–time graph geometry, and algebra. When useful, show one of the other equations only as an optional alternative labelled **Method 2**. Do not present Method 2 as required memorised knowledge.

## Calibrate generated question format and difficulty

- Treat **Section A as the multiple-choice section**. Use it to calibrate MCQ
  stem length, four-option structure, and misconception-based distractors. Do
  not use "Section A" as a synonym for easy; Section A can contain MCQs at
  different cognitive difficulties.
- Treat **easy** as foundation level: test one definition, one direct graph
  reading, one gradient, one simple area, or one application of
  \(a=(v-u)/t\). Keep a written question to one main reasoning step and normally
  1–3 marks.
- Treat **medium** as Section B structured level: use one object or one clearly
  scaffolded context with linked subparts such as describe motion, calculate a
  gradient, calculate one or more graph areas, and find an average quantity.
  Make the route visible through the subparts and normally allocate 3–6 marks.
- Treat **hard** as Section C free-response level: use one coherent multi-part
  context, reduced scaffolding, at least three linked reasoning steps, and
  normally 7–12 marks.
- Require every hard non-MCQ question to contain:
  1. at least one explicit **calculate**, **determine**, or **show that** part;
  2. at least one explicit **explain**, **justify**, or **describe how/why**
     part;
  3. a later part that uses, compares, checks, or interprets earlier work.
- Do not create difficulty merely with larger numbers, tedious arithmetic,
  memorisation of extra equations, or calculus.

## Use Section C hard-question patterns

Choose and rotate among these patterns:

1. **Two-object velocity–time graph:** calculate each cumulative displacement
   and their separation at a selected time, then explain how the separation
   changes using relative velocity. Do not confuse equal instantaneous
   velocities with the objects meeting.
2. **Multi-stage graph:** calculate gradients, several areas, and an average
   speed or velocity, then explain the physical meaning of a section, sign, or
   turning point.
3. **Rebound or direction change:** calculate speed change and signed velocity
   change, or construct a vector-difference diagram, then explain why direction
   changes the answer.
4. **Reverse or target problem:** use area and gradient information to determine
   an unknown time, speed, or distance, then justify whether an object reaches a
   target or compare two journeys.
5. **Representation change:** move between a table, motion description, and
   velocity–time graph; calculate a required quantity and explain whether the
   proposed graph or conclusion is physically consistent.

Make the hard calculation challenging through selecting the correct regions,
signs, reference time, or pair of objects. Make the explanation test graph
meaning or relative motion rather than merely asking for a definition.

## Structure answers

1. State the known values and define any unknown.
2. Use \(a=(v-u)/t\) to connect velocity, acceleration, and time when needed.
3. Use velocity–time graph area as the primary way to connect motion with displacement or distance.
4. Form and solve an equation for reverse or harder variants.
5. Give the answer with units and a brief physical interpretation where useful.
6. Add **Method 2 (optional)** with another constant-acceleration equation only when it genuinely helps.

Never make a valid O Level solution appear dependent on calculus or on memorising all five equations.
